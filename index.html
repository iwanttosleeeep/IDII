How to read this

Each range is where 8 in 10 people with this profile would be expected to score. A narrow range that sits away from the average means background is informative for that measure. A wide range that covers most of the grey band means background tells us little, and the only way to know where someone stands is to ask them the questions.

Across the survey, background and contact explain only a small part of the differences between people. Support for inclusion is the measure background predicts best, and even there it accounts for under a fifth of the differences. For most other measures the range barely moves whatever profile is entered. This matches the main finding of the report: attitudes need to be measured directly rather than inferred from who someone is.

The tag next to each measure gives how much background tells us about it, based on testing the predictions on respondents the model had not seen.

  • Some guide: background explains 8% or more of the differences between people.
  • Weak guide: 3% to 8%.
  • No real guide: under 3%. Treat the range as close to the whole sample's.
Method

One model per measure, fitted to the 360 respondents who completed the questionnaire. Each is a ridge regression on the inputs on the left, which shrinks small and unreliable effects towards zero so that the sample size does not produce overconfident predictions. Ranges are set from errors in 10-fold cross-validation, so they reflect how well the model predicts people it was not trained on; in that test, 80% of respondents fell inside their predicted range on every measure. Scores run from 1 to 5, and reverse-worded measures are scored so that a higher number means a more inclusive position, as in the report.
